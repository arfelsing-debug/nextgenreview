# Personalised Report Specification

## Purpose

The report converts 48 responses into a practical portrait of readiness. It must never imply that there is one correct successor profile or that the participant is obliged to enter the family enterprise.

## Proposed report sequence

1. Cover: Confidential NextGen Readiness Report, using the canonical Prague barometer artwork (Dancing House upper left, Kafka head lower right, six readiness dimensions and red/amber/green indicators). The same master artwork is used for English, Czech and German reports.
2. How to read this report
3. NextGen Readiness Portrait: six dimensions
4. Three strongest foundations
5. Three areas to develop
6. Knowledge and understanding
7. Ownership and financial readiness
8. Governance, voice and decision-making
9. Independent capability and experience
10. Expectations and transition
11. Resilience and unexpected responsibility
12. Readiness signals and tensions
13. Questions worth discussing with the family
14. Twelve-month development plan
15. First Action
16. Methodology and confidentiality

## Interpretation

Do not expose a single overall readiness percentage.

Dimension and subdimension calculations are analytical inputs. Participant-facing language uses Established, Developing, Exposed and Unclear.

An 'Exposed' classification means responsibility may arrive before preparation is sufficiently established. It is not a judgement of character, intelligence or suitability.

'Unclear' is substantive information. Repeated 'I do not know' responses can indicate lack of access, communication or exposure and should not simply disappear from the report.

## Priority selection

Priority recommendations should combine:
- low or uncertain critical items;
- cross-signal rules;
- subdimension results;
- the participant's strongest established capabilities;
- practical actions achievable within 12 months.

Avoid generic recommendations. Actions should be observable and finite.

Examples include observing two board meetings, reviewing the ownership map with counsel, reading consolidated accounts with the CFO, interviewing key advisers, leading one family meeting agenda item, taking responsibility for a bounded investment decision, or creating an emergency contact and authority map.
